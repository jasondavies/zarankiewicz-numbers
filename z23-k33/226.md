# Necessary structure at 226 ones on 23 × 23

**There must be at least eight nine-one rows and eight nine-one columns.** The [225-one square](README.md) has just five of each. The following restrictions hold in either orientation. Every degree is at least nine because deleting a line leaves at most [217 ones](../z22x23-k33/218.md).

Call a degree of at least ten high. Give a column of degree $c$ weight $c-9$.

* Ten entries on a row cannot include at least five high columns and have weight sum at least nine.
* Eleven entries on a row cannot include six high columns. In particular, a row of degree $d\ge11$ meets at least $d-5$ nine-columns.

To check either assertion, mark the specified ten or eleven columns. Each of the 22 other rows gives a block. Two blocks intersect in at most two points. If two rows shared six marked columns, those columns would need at least 55 entries because at most five marked columns have degree nine. The rows could supply at most $12+21\cdot2=54$. Hence all blocks have size at most five. Their total load is at least 89 in the first case and at least 94 in the second. Each point has load at least eight.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 89 | 1,391 | 1,390 | 0 |

None of the retained projections contains eight blocks with pairwise intersections of size at most one. An extra column through the marked row would require eight such blocks. All missing columns therefore avoid that row. The border search tests all degrees from nine to 22 and the required total of 226. Missing supports are distinct because two columns sharing even seven rows could supply at most $14+21\cdot2=56$ of the required 63 entries. ## Counting nine-columns

Write $a,b$ for the counts of nine-rows and nine-columns. The total row excess above ten on rows of degree at least eleven is $a-4$, and the column excess is $b-4$.

If $b\le5$, the second mark rules out every long row. Then $a=4$, and by transposition there are no long columns either. There are nineteen ten-rows and four nine-columns with just 36 incidences between them and the full set of rows. Some ten-row meets at most one nine-column. Its other entries violate the ten-point restriction.

If $b=6$, each long row has exactly eleven entries and meets all six nine-columns. There can be at most two such rows, so $a\le6$. The previous paragraph applied in both orientations gives $a=6$. There are two eleven-rows and two eleven-columns. The two eleven-rows occupy twelve entries in the six nine-columns. Every other row meets exactly two of these columns, since the remaining 42 entries must fit into 21 rows at most twice each. Every ten-row therefore has eight high entries, all on ten-columns by the ten-point restriction. An eleven-column could use only the six nine-rows and the two eleven-rows, which is impossible.

If $b=7$, every long row uses at least six of the seven nine-columns. Three long rows would have at least four of those columns in common. Thus there are at most two long rows and their degrees are at most twelve. Since $a\ge7$, the total row excess $a-4$ is at least three. Two twelve-rows would share all seven nine-columns, whose 63 entries exceed capacity 56. Hence there is one eleven-row and one twelve-row, giving $a=7$. By transposition there is also a twelve-column. The long rows share exactly six nine-columns. Every other row meets exactly two of these six and at most one further nine-column. A ten-row therefore has at least seven high entries. If it hit the twelve-column their total weight would be at least nine, violating the ten-point restriction. Only seven nine-rows and two long rows are available to that twelve-column.

Finally, on the nine-rows and nine-columns the induced submatrix avoids an all-ones $2\times2$. Deleting those two rows and two columns would leave $226-36+4=194$ ones on the [21-square, whose maximum is 193](../z21-k33/README.md).

Every nine-row meets a nine-column and vice versa. Otherwise deleting that line leaves a 217-one rectangle with every line of degree at least nine. [Minimum-degree exclusion](../z22x23-k33/min9.md)

## A nine-row has incident weight at most ten

A nine-row cannot share six columns with any other row. The six columns would all have degree nine, since they need at most 54 entries in total. Every one of the 21 remaining rows would meet them exactly twice. Among those rows there is another nine-row, and its two common nine-columns with the original nine-row would violate the $2\times2$ deletion bound above.

Suppose a nine-row had weight at least eleven. Drop an incident nine-column, which exists by the [217-one minimum-degree exclusion](../z22x23-k33/min9.md). The remaining eight points have total load at least 75 and each has load at least eight. Every block has size at most five by the preceding paragraph. Of 306,203 eight-point projections, restoring a point of load eight and identifying relabellings gives 596 distinct nine-point projections. None accepts the fourteen missing columns at total weight 226. The square replay also checks this restriction. In particular a nine-row meeting $s$ nine-columns can carry at most $s+1$ units of excess above ten on its high columns.
