# Excluding 218 ones on 22 × 23

The maximum is **216 or 217**. The 216-one witness is stored alongside an exhaustive checker for the following exclusion. [Witness](witness-216.txt) · [Replay](verify_218.py)

## Deleting a nine-row

Assume the weight is 218. Each row has degree at least nine by the upper bound of 209 on 21 × 23. There is a nine-row since 22 tens would give 220. All columns have degree at least eight by the upper bound of 210 on the 22-square. There is at most one eight-column, since deleting two would leave 202 on 22 × 21, whose maximum is 201. Let $a$ count the nine-rows and $k$ the eight-columns. No nine-row meets an eight-column since deleting it leaves a 209-one rectangle with minimum column degree eight. [Smaller bounds](README.md) · [209-one restrictions](../z21x23-k33/six-columns.md)

Deleting any nine-row gives row deficit $D=a-1$ and leaves $q$ eight-columns. The known ranges are $4\le D\le6$ for $q=4$ and $5\le D\le7$ for $q=5$. In particular $5\le a\le8$. The long rows have degrees 11 to 13 and total excess $a-2$ above ten. If $u,v$ count twelve- and thirteen-rows, the additional restrictions are $2(D+u+3v)\le3q+3$, and $q=5$ when $v>0$. [Four-column restriction](../z21x23-k33/four-deficit-seven.md)

Call columns of degree at least ten high. Their positive excesses above nine sum to $11+k$. Each nine-row meets $q-k$ original nine-columns and $9-q+k$ high columns. Two nine-rows have at most one common original nine-column, since after deleting one row the other meets at most one residual eight-column. A high column of degree $c$ contains at least three nine-rows if $c=10$, or at least $c-6$ if $c\ge11$. Delete a nine-row avoiding the column if possible and apply the deficit bound on the surviving column. If all nine-rows hit the column, deleting one reduces both its degree and its deficit count by one. For $c=11$ in that last case the required bound follows directly from $a\ge5$. In particular every degree is at most 14. [Deficit on a high column](../z21x23-k33/eight-columns.md)

## A bound on high entries of a long row

Give a column of degree $c$ weight $c-9$. If a long row meets eleven columns of weight sum at least two, mark those columns. Their point loads in the other 21 rows total at least 90. Each load is at least seven. A projected row has size at most five since six common columns would need at least $5\cdot9+8=53$ ones, against a capacity of $12+20\cdot2=52$. Two projected rows meet in at most two points. There are 52,885 projections at the load threshold. Keep those with at most one marked eight-column and positive column weights totalling at most twelve.

There are 338 projections with no eight-column on the mark and 3,794 with one. None has seven blocks meeting pairwise in at most one point. Thus another column on the marked row is impossible, as even an eight-column needs seven more entries. With no eight-column anywhere, test twelve missing columns of degrees 9–14 at weight 218. With an unmarked eight-column, omit it and test eleven missing columns at weight 210. With a marked eight-column, test twelve missing columns at weight 218. All three searches exclude every projection. Missing supports may be taken distinct because seven common rows need at least 63 entries against capacity $14+21\cdot2=56$. [Projection and border check](verify_218.py)

A long row thus has at most one high entry, except that an eleven-row meeting the eight-column can have two. Indeed if a row of degree at least twelve meets the eight-column, omit that column when choosing the eleven largest weights. The same projection test at 210 applies on either side of the 22-square obtained by deleting the eight-column. In this square all lines have degree at least nine and at most fourteen, their positive excesses sum to twelve on each side, and there are eleven missing lines. Every line of degree at least eleven consequently meets at most one line of degree above nine.

## Finite degree counts

The replay enumerates the long row degrees $R$, the multiset $H$ of high column degrees by partitioning $11+k$, the number $n$ of nine-rows with $q=5$, and which long rows hit the eight-column. Write $b=23-k-|H|$ for the number of nine-columns and $T=22-a-|R|$ for the number of ten-rows. The following tests use only integer arithmetic.

* The nine-rows use $s=(5+k)a-n$ high entries and $9a-s$ nine-column entries. On nine-columns their pair count is at most $\binom a2$. Across all columns their triple count is at most $2\binom a3$. For a lower bound distribute each total as evenly as possible. On the high columns start at the individual lower bounds on the numbers of nine-rows and increment a least-loaded column at each step. This minimizes the triple count because the increments of $\binom t3$ are nondecreasing.
* Let $x_i$ indicate whether the long row of degree $d_i$ hits the eight-column, and $h=\sum x_i$. Then $\sum_i(d_i-10)x_i\le3$, by deleting any nine-row and using the net excess bound on an eight-column. For each nine-row deletion, long row $i$ must cover at least $d_i-8-x_i$ of the $q-k$ newly low columns. The lower bound on the intersection of two such subsets must be at most two since all the columns in the intersection also contain the deleted row.
* The long rows have at most $C=|R|+\sum_{i:d_i=11}x_i$ high entries altogether. On the $b$ nine-columns they therefore have at least $\sum R-h-C$ entries. The balanced lower pair and triple counts on those columns must be at most $5\binom{|R|}{2}$ and $2\binom{|R|}{3}$. Six shared nine-columns would need 54 entries against capacity 52. On the high columns the ten-rows need at least $\sum H-s-C$ entries. Their balanced lower triple count together with the $n\binom{4+k}{3}+(a-n)\binom{5+k}{3}$ triples from the nine-rows is at most $2\binom{|H|}{3}$. [Long-row and net-excess bounds at 209](../z21x23-k33/eight-columns.md)

Seven patterns survive when permutations of equal-degree rows are identified. All have $k=1$ and $h=3$. In the 22-square obtained by deleting that eight-column, the number of nine-rows is $A=a+8-h$. The two largest columns have degrees $c,d\ge11$, so they contain at least $c-1$ and $d-1$ of the nine-rows. Any two columns in this square share at most five rows, since six rows have degree at least nine but two columns sharing them could supply at most $12+20\cdot2=52$ entries. Thus $c+d-2\le A+5$ is necessary. Every surviving pattern violates this inequality.
