# At least six eight-columns at 193 on 20 × 22

Every 20 × 22 matrix with 193 ones and no all-ones 3 × 3 has at least six columns of degree eight. The row and column minima are eight by the upper bounds of 185 on 19 × 22 and 20 × 21. The former follows by column deletion from 177 on 19 × 21. Five eight-columns are necessary since there are 22 columns. If exactly five occur, the other seventeen columns all have degree nine. Call them high.

Two rows cannot share six columns. Such columns would require at least 49 entries since at least one has degree nine, against capacity 48. An eleven-row meets exactly six high columns; it must contain all five eight-columns. Indeed marking eleven entries, seven or more high, gives nineteen blocks on eleven points with total load at least 84 and maximum size five. The [nineteen-block bound](../z20x21-k33/rare-rows.md) excludes this. A longer row could supply such a mark, so the maximum row degree is eleven. A ten-row meets at most eight high columns: total mark load at least 79 with every point load seven or eight has no projection. The replay regenerates the full catalogue at load at least 78.

Write x,y,z,w for the numbers of rows of degrees 8,9,10,11. Then y=7−2x+w and z=13+x−2w. At most two eleven-rows can contain the five eight-columns. Suppose first that every ten-row meets at least three eight-columns. Those columns carry forty entries, so 3z+5w=39+3x−w is at most forty. Two eleven-rows would already use every triple of eight-columns twice, ruling out any ten-row. For w=1 the count forces x=0, z=11. Only ten distinct triples of eight-columns are left for the eleven ten-rows, again impossible. Thus w=x=0, leaving seven nine-rows and thirteen ten-rows. The [627-mark search](three-profile-proof.md) excludes this exact profile when all ten-rows meet at least three eight-columns. That step uses only the stated degrees and the forbidden submatrix condition.

Otherwise mark a ten-row meeting eight high columns. The score d+W, with W the number of high columns on the row, has maximum eighteen: eight- and nine-rows have score at most sixteen and eighteen, ten-rows at most eighteen, and eleven-rows exactly seventeen. The marked row attains eighteen. Its ten point loads on the nineteen outside rows are two sevens and eight eights. The 882 canonical projections all fail completion to 193. The search permits degree-eight rows and sets the sum of squared column excesses above eight to seventeen, forcing exactly the five eights and seventeen nines. Run `python3 verify_six_low_columns.py --jobs 4`. [Replay](verify_six_low_columns.py)

In particular a 201-one matrix on 20 × 23 needs at least seven eight-columns. Each column has degree at least eight by the deletion upper bound of 193 on 20 × 22. At least one has degree eight, and deleting it exposes six others. In a 201-one matrix on 21 × 22 an eight-row avoids all eight-columns, since deleting that row and an incident eight-column would exceed 185 on 20 × 21. If there are q eight-columns, deleting an eight-row therefore shows that it meets at least 6−q columns of degree nine. These restrictions apply to the smaller matrices obtained during the 217 search.
