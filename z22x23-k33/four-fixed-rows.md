# An order-three symmetry at 217 must fix four rows and five columns

Suppose a 217-one 22 × 23 matrix avoids an all-ones 3 × 3 and has an order-three row-and-column symmetry. The [orbit count](odd-symmetry.md) leaves four fixed rows and either two or five fixed columns. The following exhaustion excludes the case of two fixed columns. The case with five fixed columns is covered by a [separate exhaustion](no-odd-symmetry.md).

## The fixed part when there are two fixed columns

The one or two columns of degree eight must be fixed. The matrix has at most two eight-rows as well: deleting one leaves 209 on 21 × 23, which has at most one eight-row. Thus every moving row has degree at least nine. Removing all eight-columns leaves every row with at least eight entries. A fixed row has at most one remaining fixed column and therefore meets at least three whole moving column orbits. In particular every fixed row also has degree at least nine. [Minimum degrees after deletion](eight-column-count.md) · [Eight-row bound at 209](../z21x23-k33/README.md)

A degree-eight fixed column contains two fixed rows, since its other entries come in threes. The fixed corner weight is congruent to 217 modulo three, so the other fixed column also contains two fixed rows. Up to permutation there are three corner patterns, according as the two pairs of rows coincide, meet in one row, or are disjoint.

Each of the seven moving column orbits touches at most two fixed rows. The same pair cannot be used twice: six moving columns require at least 54 entries, while two rows containing them and twenty others can supply only 52. There are six possible pairs. If all six occur, the last orbit touches zero or one fixed row. If five occur, the two remaining orbits must touch the endpoints of the missing pair separately, since each fixed row needs at least three orbits. Fewer than five pairs would leave fewer than twelve incidences. This gives eleven labelled choices for these strips.

Each of the six moving row orbits can touch neither fixed column, either one, or both. Enumerate these choices as a multiset. Two fixed columns intersect at most six times: seven common rows of degree at least nine need 63 entries, against capacity 56. When both columns have degree eight their intersection is at most five by the [short-overlap proof](short-overlap.md). The 18 × 21 moving submatrix has weight at most 170. [18-row upper bound](../z20x21-k33/core-search.md)

## Finishing the search

A moving row orbit and a moving column orbit meet in a circulant 3 × 3 block. Its row sum is 0, 1 or 2. The total of these block sums is forced by weight 217. Enumerate the possible words of block sums on each row and column orbit. Enforce the line degrees and the triple constraints involving fixed lines. In a size-two block a pair from the same orbit has one common position. In a size-one block it has none. Each group of word domains must reach the required total, and a block value must occur in the domains on both sides. These preliminary checks leave 394 sets of domains.

The completion program chooses the six row words and checks whether their prefixes still occur in the corresponding column domains. For three distinct moving row groups with block sizes a,b,c in a given column block, that block contributes abc/9 to the mean number of common positions over the choices of row representatives. Sum these means and the common fixed entries; the result must be at most two. The column test likewise considers every triple of distinct actual columns, allowing repeated moving groups and averaging over their distinct representatives. Within a group of three, two sampled positions both fall in a block of size two with probability one third.

There is an additional exact two-orbit test. In each shared column block normalize the first moving row orbit by a rotation, then try each relative rotation of the second. Check all three relative row positions against the pair-intersection bound, all triples containing two representatives from one orbit, and all triples containing a row from each orbit and a fixed row. A pair of rows can share six columns only if both degree-eight columns occur among the six, since their total degree must be at most 52. Rotating the labels of the second row orbit permits fixing its first relative block shift. Equal fixed-incidence types can be ordered lexicographically on both axes; a row-major lexicographic minimum satisfies both orderings.

All **394 sets of domains** fail. These are necessary tests, so exhausting them excludes the symmetry. The positive control recovers the quotient of a known 216-one rectangle using the order-three action on the 225-one square. Run `python3 verify_order_three.py --jobs 4` with NumPy and a C++20 compiler.
