# Excluding 209 ones on 21 × 23

The maximum on 21 × 23 is either 207 or 208. In particular every 217-one candidate on 22 × 23 has minimum row degree nine. Deleting two nine-rows from the [225-one square](../z23-k33/witness-225.txt) supplies the lower bound.

Suppose there are 209 ones. Write q for the number of degree-eight columns, called low. The earlier reduction gives four or five of them, and every column has degree at least eight. Removing any low column leaves 201 on 21 × 22, where every row has degree at least nine. Consequently there are no eight-rows, and every nine-row avoids every low column. Let D be the number of nine-rows. [Column counts](six-columns.md) · [201-one row minimum](../z21x22-k33/parent-minimum.md)

Give each high column of degree c the weight c−9. The sum of the weights is q+2. Every positive-weight column contains at least three nine-rows. For degree ten this is the three-unit deficit bound. At larger degrees every choice of eleven incident rows has at least five units of deficit below ten, and only nine-rows contribute deficit. On the other hand, a nine-row meets nine high columns in the 201-one rectangle obtained by deleting a low column. Their degrees are unchanged, so its incident weight is at most three. Counting weighted incidences with nine-rows gives
$$3(q+2)\le 3D.$$
[Ten-column deficit](eight-columns.md) · [Eleven-row deficit](parent-extensions.md) · [Nine high entries](../z21x22-k33/row-excess.md)

On each low column the net sum of incident row degrees minus ten is at most three. A long row of degree 10+s, with s positive, meets at least s+2 low columns. No nine-row meets a low column, so each unit of positive row surplus is counted at least three times across the low columns. Since the total weight is one below 210, the positive row surplus is D−1. Hence
$$3(D-1)\le 3q.$$
This requires D at most q+1, in contradiction with D at least q+2. [Low-column capacity](eight-columns.md) · [Long rows](README.md)

Any 217-one 22 × 23 matrix therefore contains at least three nine-rows. Deleting any one yields 208 on 21 × 23. If the 21 × 23 value is 207, the 22 × 23 value is necessarily 216.
