# Exact square and near-square Zarankiewicz numbers for avoiding a 3 × 3 all-ones submatrix

Let $z(m,n)$ be the largest number of ones in an $m$ by $n$ binary matrix in which no three rows have three common ones. Transposition gives $z(m,n)=z(n,m)$. The following six consecutive square maxima have explicit witnesses and computer-assisted upper bounds.

| $n$ | 17 | 18 | 19 | 20 | 21 | 22 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $z(n,n)$ | 138 | 150 | 164 | 180 | 193 | 210 |
| $z(n-1,n)$ | 132 | 143 | 156 | 171 | 185 | 201 |

Two further rectangles used in this work have $z(16,18)=137$ and $z(19,21)=177$. Wei previously established the values on 16 × 17 and 16 × 18. Table 4 of Collins, Riasanovsky, Wallace and Radziszowski gives upper bounds of 141, 148 and 156 for 17 × 17, 17 × 18 and 18 × 18, respectively. A matrix in the repository certifies each lower bound by inspecting its row triples. [Wei's proof and data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24553293) · [Collins et al., Table 4](https://www.cs.rit.edu/~spr/PUBL/crwr16.pdf) · [Witnesses and proof directories](README.md#results)

## How the upper bounds fit together

Mark a row of degree $t$ and take its columns as $t$ labelled points. The other rows give subsets of those points. Any two of those subsets share at most two points, since the marked row contains every point. Near the target weight, the minimum column degrees also give a lower bound on how often each point must occur. This makes it possible to enumerate short lists of projections up to permutation of rows and points. For each representative, the remaining columns must have the required total weight and preserve the triple condition. A proof must cover every possible choice of degrees and include at least one mark from every hypothetical matrix.

* **17 and 18.** The 17 × 17 exclusion feeds the 17 × 18 proof, which in turn feeds the 18 × 18 proof. The extremal 18 × 19 problem illustrates how to use almost-complete matrices. At weight 157 one can force columns of degree seven, remove them, enumerate the denser part, and test whether all the removed columns can be restored together. [17 × 17](z17-k33/README.md) · [17 × 18](z17x18-k33/README.md) · [18 × 18](z18-k33/README.md) · [18 × 19](z18x19-k33/marked-search.md)
* **19 and 20.** For a square of weight $w$, summing the weights after all single-row deletions gives $(n-1)w\le n z(n-1,n)$. At $n=19$ and $20$ this proves the upper bounds 164 and 180 directly from 156 and 171. The 20 × 20 witness is cyclic and has exactly nine ones on every line. [19 × 19](z19-k33/README.md) · [19 × 20](z19x20-k33/README.md) · [20 × 20](z20-k33/README.md)
* **21.** A 186-one matrix on 20 × 21 would have an eight-one row leaving 178 on 19 × 21. A 194-one square would have two parallel eight-one lines leaving that same weight, possibly after transposition. A single exhaustion on 19 × 21 therefore gives both upper bounds. It checks 515,390 marked projections across the cases of zero, one and two seven-one columns. The 193-one square supplies all three witnesses by deletion. [Degree reductions](z20x21-k33/weight-186.md) · [Exhaustion at 178](z19x21-k33/README.md) · [21 × 21](z21-k33/README.md)
* **22.** Adding an eight-one column to the 193-one square gives 201 on 21 × 22. Adding a nine-one row gives 210 on 22 × 22. In the opposite direction, a hypothetical 202-one rectangle forces a ten-one row meeting at least two units of column excess above degree nine. Its projection has only 159 possibilities, and none completes. Row deletion now gives $w\le\lfloor22\cdot201/21\rfloor=210$ for the square. [Rectangular exclusion and replay](z21x22-k33/README.md) · [22 × 22](z22-k33/README.md)

The computations use smaller Zarankiewicz bounds as inputs. Each linked proof identifies its dependencies. Replaying the final 159 cases checks the step from the two preceding maxima to the last two values. Reproducing the entire series also requires replaying the earlier exclusions.

## Beyond 22

The rectangle satisfies $216\le z(22,23)\le217$, and the square has $z(23,23)=225$. Eight orbits of cells give the 225-one square, and removing a nine-one row gives the rectangle. Row deletion from $z(21,23)\le209$ gives an initial rectangular upper bound of 218 and an initial square upper bound of 227. Eleven-point projections and degree counts exclude 218 in the rectangle. [Rectangular upper bound](z22x23-k33/218.md) An exhaustive check of the nine-rows independently excludes 227 in the square. [Square upper bound](z23-k33/227.md) The joint replay reconstructs the square, checks both witnesses and exhausts the 159 projections needed for the auxiliary upper bound. [Construction](z23-k33/README.md) · [Proof and replay](z22x23-k33/README.md)

A 209-one matrix on 21 × 23 must contain an extremal 177-one matrix on 21 × 19 that accepts four simultaneous eight-one columns. Such a parent has zero or one eight-column.
