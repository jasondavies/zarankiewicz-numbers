# Every extremal 21 × 22 matrix contains an extremal 21 × 21 square

**Every 201-one matrix on 21 × 22 avoiding an all-ones 3 × 3 has a column of degree eight.** Removing it leaves a 193-one square. This holds independently of whether the rectangle can be extended.

Every column has degree at least eight by the [21 × 21 maximum](../z21-k33/README.md). Suppose they all have degree at least nine. Two rows cannot share six columns: these need 54 entries while the two rows and nineteen others supply at most 50. A row with eleven entries would give twenty blocks of size at most five on eleven marked points, all point loads at least eight, contrary to the [eleven-point lemma](../z21-k33/README.md). Thus the row degrees are at most ten. The deficit from 21 tens is nine, so there are at most nine rows shorter than ten.

Write a column degree as $9+e$, with $\sum e=3$. Let $H$ sum the incident excesses on a row. A ten-row with $H\ge2$ has ten marked points of loads at least eight and at most eleven, total at least 82. The 20 blocks meet pairwise in at most two points. The 12 missing columns avoid the marked row and have degree nine or ten since at most one excess unit remains. Distinct supports suffice: if two columns coincided on at least nine rows, the remaining twenty columns would be allowed at most two entries each on these rows, which need at least $9(8-2)=54$ further entries. Here every row has degree at least eight by the 20 × 22 upper bound of 193. The 159 canonical projections at load at least 82 admit **no completion**.

A column of degree eleven or more would have to avoid all ten-rows and cannot fit on the nine shorter rows. Hence the columns are nineteen nines and three tens. Each ten-column meets a ten-row, so a ten-row of $H=1$ exists. Its score $2d+H=21$ is maximal, where $d$ is row degree: all shorter rows have $H\le3$. There are 5,487 projections with one point of load nine and the other nine of load eight. Exhaust the remaining columns with total 201 and score at most 21 on every row, allowing all row degrees. The marked-core checker uses baseline eight, so the score in its notation is $d+\sum_{j\text{ incident}}(c_j-8)=2d+H$. The column sum of squared excesses above eight is 31. All 5,487 projections give **zero cores**. [Replay](../z22x23-k33/verify_two_columns.py)

## Consequence for 22 × 23

A 217-one rectangle has exactly one or two eight-columns. In the two-column case, delete both and transpose to obtain 201 on 21 × 22. In the one-column case delete it, leaving a 209-one square. If this square has an eight-row, delete that row as well, again obtaining 201 on 21 × 22. Both branches therefore contain an extremal 193-one square. In the second branch the reconstruction must add a row and a column to the 201-one rectangle, whereas the first adds two rows. The exceptions to this common reduction are the two [remaining minimum-degree-nine square-parent profiles](../z22x23-k33/square-parent.md).

Conversely, neither exceptional family can contain a 193-one 21 × 21 submatrix. Such a submatrix deletes one row and two columns. If the unique eight-column is among them, the remaining deletion takes a row and column from the 209-one parent, removing at least $9+9-1=17$ more cells. If both deleted columns have degree at least nine, the full row also has degree at least nine, and the deletion removes at least $9+9+9-2=25$ cells from 217. In both cases at most 192 remain. Thus the presence of a 193-one square exactly separates these two profiles from all the other surviving branches.

The eleven-line exceptional profile has since been excluded. Every surviving 217-one matrix without an extremal 193-one subsquare must have the nine-and-ten square parent. [Final profile reduction](../z22x23-k33/last-profile.md)
