# Reducing 217 on 22 × 23 to 177 on 19 × 21

Every 217-one example would contain an extremal 201-one matrix on 21 × 22. The following computational reduction finds an extremal 177-one matrix inside it. The 216–217 bounds are unchanged. [201-one reduction](../z22x23-k33/last-profile.md)

## The counterexample to containment

Suppose a 201-one rectangle contains no 177-one rectangle on 19 × 21 in either orientation. It has at least two eight-columns. Three give the desired parent immediately. Delete its two eight-columns, called C and D, to obtain B of weight 185 on 21 × 20. Restoring either column gives a 193-one square. Each such square has an eight-row and an eight-column. Two parallel eight-lines would give the desired 177, so each square has exactly one of each. Every row of B has degree at least seven. [Eight-line theorem](../z21-k33/eight-lines.md)

## Degrees in B

For any 185-one 21 × 20 matrix with column degrees at least nine and row degrees at least seven, the rows have degree at most nine. Two rows share at most five columns by the six-column capacity bound of 50, and a row of degree eleven or more contradicts the twenty-block eleven-point lemma. For a ten-row let H be its incident column excess above nine. There are five units of excess in total. Mark the ten-row and project the other twenty rows. At H at least two, their total load is at least 82 and no point exceeds load 13. The 159 projections yield no completion using ten more columns of degrees nine to twelve. At H equal to one, the 5,487 projections yield no completion with missing degrees nine to thirteen. If every ten-row has H equal to zero, a ten-row maximizes 5d+H at 50. The 79,801 projections of ten loads eight yield zero completions at weight 185 across 558,607 choices of mark and degree profile. These checks allow squared column excess between 5 and 25. [Eleven-point lemma](../z21-k33/README.md)

The total row deficit below nine is therefore four. Two columns share at most five rows, since six rows need at least 50 ones against capacity 48. On eleven points of any column of degree at least eleven, the other nineteen columns would have load at least 84, impossible by the nineteen-block eleven-point lemma. Hence there are five ten-columns and fifteen nine-columns. Every ten-column meets total row deficit at least three. Otherwise, marking it gives ten points on nineteen blocks, maximum point load eight and total at least 78. All 920 projections fail the eleven-row completion test, even when missing degrees six to nine are permitted. Missing row supports are distinct by the bound on shared columns. [Nineteen-block lemma](../z20x21-k33/rare-rows.md)

This argument applies both to B and to the transpose of either 193-one square after removal of its unique eight-row. Each square has one eight, fifteen nines and five tens in both orientations. Its five ten-rows meet its eight-column, and its five ten-columns meet its eight-row. These are the same five ten-columns in B for both squares. No two rows can cover all five ten-columns, since 50 entries would have capacity only 48. Hence both squares have the same eight-row R.

## Two positions of R

If R avoids C and D, B has four eight-rows and seventeen nine-rows. Both C and D contain the three other short rows. Each contains five further rows that become tens. The eight columns of R include all five tens. Each ten-column meets at least two of the three other short rows, by the deficit bound. Each pair among the three can occur in at most two ten-columns, since R meets all five. A ten-column containing all three would spend at least seven pair incidences against capacity six. Thus the five ten-columns meet exactly two each, with pair multiplicities 2,2,1. The transposed argument in each square gives the same condition for its five ten-rows on the three nine-columns of R.

Mark R. The generator on eight points, twenty outside rows, load at least 69 and point loads at least eight gives 30,260 marks with the required degree histogram. Choosing the three short rows and one extension with these pair constraints gives 13,704 choices. Grouping by the mark and the three short rows gives 4,587 groups. No group has two distinct extensions whose common support meets each marked column at most twice. C and D cannot coincide since identical eight-columns exceed the pair bound. [Disjoint search](disjoint_frame.cpp)

If R meets both C and D, it has degree seven in B. Two further rows A and B' have degree eight and eighteen have degree nine. Both extensions contain R, A and B'. The seven columns of R contain five tens and two nines, X and Y. Each ten-column meets A or B' by the deficit bound. The traces of A and B' on the seven points are disjoint because the three rows already share C and D. Each trace has size at most three because six shared columns in the 201 rectangle would need at least 52 ones against capacity 50. The dual deficit bound in each square requires every one of the five extra rows of its extension to meet X or Y.

First mark the five tens and X. From the 259,204 six-point marks of loads 9,9,9,9,9,8, adding Y on eight outside rows produces 2,375,442 labelled choices. The pair and triple conditions on A, B', C and D leave 133 choices across 64 starting marks. For each, the nine columns meeting R are now fixed, and all thirteen missing columns have degree nine. On each outside row their required total is nine minus its seven-point trace size, minus one if it is A or B'. Every row in the full rectangle has degree at least nine, so no two columns may share six rows. Enumerating compatible nine-subsets with the required row totals gives zero completions on all 133 choices. [Incident search](incident_frame.cpp) · [Exact completion](complete_frame.cpp)

## Remaining search

Orient the 177 parent as 21 × 19. Reversing the deletions gives the four sequences CCCRR, CCCRC, CCRCC and CCRCR, where C adds a column and R adds a row, each with eight ones at the time it is added. The first three additions recover the 201 rectangle, up to transposition. The last two recover 217. The two stored 177 parents both fail. With ordered additions, the standard witness reaches the fourth addition 54 ways in each sequence and none finishes. The parent cropped from the 225 square reaches the second addition four ways and none reaches the third. The counts include different orders for the same lines. Other parents remain unclassified. [Standard parent](../z19x21-k33/witness-177.txt) · [Cropped parent](../z21x23-k33/parent-from-225.txt)
